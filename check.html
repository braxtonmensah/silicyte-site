02 What each check catches

Why these four and not others.

Because these are the four that got us. Every one of them passed a check we thought was adequate, and every one of them changed a number we had already written down.

CheckThe failure it catchesWhat it cost us
LEAK A held-out molecule has a near-twin in training. The clustering in common use documents itself as single linkage but compares each sequence only to cluster representatives, first match wins, so transitively similar pairs land in different folds. 20.1% of MIC and 24.6% of HC50 held-out rows, while the standard family-overlap check passed 826 of 826
FLOOR The reported effect is smaller than the resolution of the instrument that read it. A gain measured over k kept molecules carries an irreducible floor of sd·√(2/k). six of our own “the independent metric did not move” readings sat below their own instrument's resolution
TIES The rank correlation is not tie-corrected. argsort(argsort(x)) is an ordinal rank, and it biases every correlation once the target has repeated values, which activity labels always do. every Spearman in one of our lanes needed recomputing
SPLIT The same molecule appears on both sides, by string identity, before similarity is even considered. The cheapest possible defect and the easiest to acquire when two databases are merged. one public compilation already contained 100% of another, which is how a “new data source” turns out to be the old one
The leak does not flatter all models equally, which is the dangerous part. A large model can memorise a near-twin's label where a 36-feature linear model cannot, so the leak inflates exactly the models people are most excited about. Measured inflation on our own data reaches +0.065 on the safety axis. If you are comparing a protein language model against a simple baseline on a leaky split, the comparison is not measuring what you think.

A pass here is not a certificate. These four are necessary and nowhere near sufficient: they say nothing about whether your target is well specified, and on that point see the data page, where we measure that 74.2% of the variance in the standard antimicrobial target is which bacterium was tested rather than anything about the peptide.

Two things worth saying plainly

We have no laboratory and no experimental result of our own. Every number on this site is computational, and these are computational checks. It can tell you your reported gain is an artifact. It cannot tell you your molecule works.

And we would rather be corrected than cited. If a check here is wrong, the source is one file and the argument is in the open. Send it.
