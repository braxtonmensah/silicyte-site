02 What each check catches

What each diagnostic reports.

Every check below exists because it caught something in our own work first. The research record documents each one, including the results it forced us to withdraw.

DiagnosticPurposeWhat a finding means
LEAKCompare peptide similarity across the designated training and test sets.A held-out row has a near-twin in training, so its score is partly recall.
SPREADDescribe the distribution of supplied predictions.Your reported effect is smaller than the resolution floor of the set it was read off.
TIESCompare tie-corrected rank correlation with an ordinal-rank calculation.Your rank statistic moves when ties are corrected, so repeated labels are biasing it.
SPLITCheck exact sequence overlap across designated training and test sets.The same sequence appears on both sides of the split.
Sequence overlap can affect model comparisons. Our historical evaluation recorded score inflation of up to 0.065 on the safety endpoint after a split defect. The size and direction of an overlap effect depend on the model and evaluation. Corrected splits and retraining are needed to assess the impact on a particular result.

Limits, stated once

These four checks describe the table you supplied. They do not certify a model, establish biological activity, or replace experimental validation. Specifically:

  • LEAK samples pairs on large files, so a run without a finding is inconclusive rather than clean.
  • SPREAD is descriptive. It is a resolution floor, not an experimental error bar and not a significance test.
  • TIES is undefined when every prediction is identical.
  • SPLIT catches exact duplicates only, so passing it does not rule out leakage by similarity. That is what LEAK is for.
  • None of them can tell you whether the prediction target matches the decision you actually care about. The dataset analysis shows why pooled activity labels make that harder than it looks.
