Company

Silicyte

We design de novo peptides and protein binders, and we build the instruments that say whether a design result is real. The second part came out of the first: we kept finding that our own encouraging numbers did not survive a control.

What we are building is a calibrated selection rule for designed molecules. One that is validated against held-out laboratory data, that measurably raises the hit rate of a fixed-size candidate set, and that ships with its code, its pre-registrations and its failed predictions public so the result can be attacked.

01 Why this matters commercially

The expensive step is deciding which molecules to make.

Designing candidates computationally is cheap and getting cheaper. Making and testing them is not. Every pipeline therefore ends in a ranking step that decides which handful goes to the bench, and that step is almost always trusted rather than measured.

Our own experiments say that trust is misplaced more often than the field assumes. A scoring model we deliberately crippled produced a larger reported improvement than the intact one, which can only happen if the reported quantity is the model's error rather than the molecules' quality. If that generalises even partially, a large share of the bench budget spent on computationally ranked candidates is being allocated by noise.

That is the problem we are equipped to work on, because we run the full design stack ourselves and because the instruments already exist in working form.

02 What we do not claim

The honest ledger.

The first thing a careful reader wants from a company like this is the list of things it cannot support. Here it is, before the list of things it can.

Not done

No laboratory validation of our own

Every number we publish is computational. We have no laboratory and claim no experimental result. Outside measurement reaches us only through challenge organisers who make and test selected entries.

Withdrawn

Our loop does not yet make molecules an independent model rates better

Across six repeated runs the real loop sits about 14% below its own scrambled control on the independent check, with an error bar a third that size. Our earlier headline to the contrary is withdrawn.

Resolved

The leaking data split is fixed, and the result survived it

We found on 2026-10-14 that our own cross-validation was leaking, rebuilt it from scratch, and re-measured everything. The scoring-model result survived and improved: 0.5356 on potency against 0.4502 for the previous approach, with a scrambled-label control at -0.0228. We also found that a hyperparameter step we thought was best practice was costing us 0.038, and removed it.

Stage

Research stage, bootstrapped, not incorporated

Silicyte was founded in October 2026 and is independent and bootstrapped. There is no legal entity yet, no outside funding and no revenue. Computational candidates are not medicines, and we do not describe them as such.
