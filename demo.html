Why the two numbers disagree.

A design loop has one obvious way to make its score go up, and it is not the way you want. Instead of finding better molecules, it can find the places where its own scoring model is wrong, and pile into them. The score rises, the molecules do not improve, and nothing in the loop's own output can tell the difference.

That is why the Break the scorer on purpose switch exists. It swaps in a model we starved of 80% of its training families, which is strictly worse at predicting anything. If the reported improvement measured molecule quality, a worse scorer would have to report less. On our cluster it reported more, by a wide margin, and you can reproduce that here in about ten seconds.

Honest about the differences from our cluster runs: this page uses a simple composition-based generator rather than our trained one, keeps tens of molecules instead of hundreds, and runs the instance-based meter against a trimmed bank so it fits in a browser. The models are real and so is the direction of the effect. The exact magnitudes on our cluster are on the science page, with their error bars.
