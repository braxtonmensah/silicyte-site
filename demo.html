Try it

Give it a peptide. Watch what we actually do with it.

Four steps, in this tab, on public data. The last one is the argument this company is built on, and it is the step everybody else skips.

Step 01
Read

What the molecule is.

Length, net charge, and the hydrophobic moment: how strongly the greasy residues fall on one face when the chain is wound as a helix. That lopsidedness is the mechanism the film on the home page is showing, and it is computed here from the sequence alone.

Step 02
Search

Who it is related to.

Every labelled peptide we hold, scored by sequence identity against yours. This is the step that decides whether a prediction is worth anything, and the step whose absence we found in the standard evaluation.

Step 03
Domain

Whether we are allowed to predict.

Our own fitted model will return a number for any string you give it. Whether that number means anything depends entirely on step 02, so the verdict comes before the number, not after it.

Step 04
Spread

Why one peptide is not one number.

Here is what the nearest labelled relative actually measured, organism by organism, in the public record. Across the whole set the median peptide's MIC moves 6.1x between organisms. Our recorded decomposition attributed 74.2% of resolvable variance to organism differences; that does not establish a universal noise ceiling.

• What this is

And what it is not.

The model is the real one: ridge weights fitted on our cluster and shipped in models.json, averaged over a penalty grid rather than selected on inner folds. Its measured out-of-fold accuracy on leak-free folds is Spearman 0.4502 for the descriptor set and 0.5356 fine-tuned, against a label whose own reproducibility caps any model at 0.9387. Those numbers, and the folds, are on the data page.

The measured values come from GRAMPA, unmodified peptides in micromolar only, reduced to a median per peptide and organism. It is public data and you should cite it rather than us. Nothing here is uploaded and nothing here is a wet-lab result: we have no laboratory, and a predicted MIC is not a measured one.
