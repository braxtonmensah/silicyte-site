Silicyte

Run the experiment in silico first.

Silicyte builds AI models that predict how human cells respond when a gene is knocked down, so drug-discovery teams can rank targets before they spend a lab cycle on them.

The problem

A genome-wide CRISPR screen measures the effect of thousands of genetic perturbations, but each one is slow and expensive, and it only covers the cell types that were actually run. Most cell lines and most target combinations will never be screened in the lab.

What we build

Virtual cell models

Models that take a cell line's unperturbed state and predict its single-cell transcriptomic response to a gene knockdown.

Transfer across cell types

Learning from public Perturb-seq atlases so a prediction can reach a cell line that was never screened.

Measured, not claimed

Every model is scored on held-out perturbations with pre-registered tests before we trust it.

Where we are

Silicyte is an early-stage company founded in 2026 in Bloomington, Indiana. Our models are currently being developed and benchmarked in the Arc Institute Virtual Cell Challenge.
