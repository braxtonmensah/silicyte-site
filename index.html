00 What you are looking at

A recorded run of our own simulation, bounded like everything else here.

Two membranes, the same peptides, and the peptides choose. The bacterial surface is negatively charged and the human one is not, so the peptide's charged face is pulled harder to the first. By the end of the run they are buried in the bacterial sheet and still sitting on top of the human one.

It is a simulation we wrote, not a measurement, and it stops short of the step that actually kills the cell. Every lipid here is tethered to its own lattice site, so the sheet springs back and no pore ever opens. Selectivity, the ratio between those two outcomes, is the number this company exists to predict, and it is measured in the science, not here.

01 The finding

A worse scoring model reported a six times larger improvement.

The theory behind these loops says the opposite should happen. A more accurate scorer picks better molecules, so it should produce a bigger gain. We ran the experiment with the scorer handicapped and with it intact, three independent runs each, and left the measuring model untouched so both were judged on the same ruler.

What a design loop is supposed to do Find molecules that kill bacteria without harming blood cells. The loop reports how much it improved that ratio. Intact scorer: 3.5x. Crippled scorer: 21x. The crippled one wins by a mile, which cannot happen if the number tracks molecule quality.
