Reference

Direct answers, with the measurement in each one.

Short self-contained answers to questions about evaluation leakage, identity thresholds, rank statistics and design-loop controls. Each one carries the figure and how it was measured, so it can be quoted without the rest of the page.

Every figure below links back to the run that produced it, through the benchmark audit, the paper or the dataset. Measurements are computational. Silicyte operates no laboratory and claims no experimental result.

How much train/test leakage is in the LRGB Peptides-func benchmark?

65.29% of LRGB Peptides-func test rows have a training sequence at a Levenshtein ratio of at least 0.80, and 7.72% share a primary sequence outright with a training row. Of those 180 exact sequence matches, only 30 are the identical molecule by SMILES. The benchmark applies no homology control: its shipped split is splits_random_stratified_peptide.pickle, a stratified random split. Measured 2026-10-17 on 2,331 test rows against 10,873 train rows, from the benchmark's own published files, whose MD5 checksums match the values hard-coded in its loader.

Does PepBenchmark's 30% identity split actually remove near-duplicates?

No, not fully. PepBenchmark's documented default hybrid_split, a k-mer split followed by MMseqs2 clustering at 30% sequence identity, still leaves 29.97% of E. coli MIC test rows and 48.56% of haemolysis HC50 test rows with a training sequence at Levenshtein ratio 0.80 or above, averaged over its five shipped seeds. A random split leaves 66.54% and 77.42%, so the control removes between half and two thirds of the contamination and does not reach zero. Exact sequence overlap is 0.00% on every dataset, strategy and seed, so its deduplication works exactly as described.

What sequence identity threshold is actually needed for a clean protein train/test split?

In the splits measured, 20% identity with whole-cluster assignment was clean and 30% identity was not. FLIP meltome clusters with mmseqs easy-cluster --min-seq-id 0.2 and assigns whole clusters 80/20 to train and test; 0.29% of its test rows retain a training sequence at Levenshtein ratio 0.80 or above. PepBenchmark clusters at 30% identity after a k-mer step and retains 29.97% to 48.56%. Both cluster and then split, and they land two orders of magnitude apart, so the threshold and the unit assigned to the split both matter.

Why does CD-HIT or MMseqs2 clustering still leave near-duplicates across a train/test split?

Because both are representative-based. Each sequence is compared to cluster representatives rather than to every cluster member, so a transitive chain where A resembles B and B resembles C but A does not resemble C gets broken, and the related sequences land on opposite sides of the split. Transitive single-linkage clustering, implemented with union-find over all pairs above the threshold, does not break those chains. Lowering the identity threshold makes chains harder to break, and assigning whole clusters rather than looser groupings stops a break from crossing the split boundary.

Can a reported improvement from a closed-loop molecular design system be trusted?

Not without a control, and the control frequently fails. In a direct intervention, degrading the scoring model on purpose by withholding 80% of its training families, while leaving the evaluation model untouched, made the reported gain 2.4 times larger: +1.3182 against +0.5421, a difference of -0.7761 with a standard error of 0.0531 and t = -14.60, over 3 seeds per rung with all six paired shuffled controls inside their pre-registered band. A model that knows less cannot design better molecules, so what such a loop reports is the scorer's error rather than molecule quality. The breeder's equation R = i*rho*sigma, the usual justification for these loops, predicts the opposite sign.

What is the smallest improvement a top-k enrichment metric can actually detect?

A gain read off k kept molecules carries an irreducible resolution floor of sd*sqrt(2/k), where sd is the per-molecule standard deviation of the measured property. With sd = 0.4562, measured over 143 winner sets, that floor is 0.0645 at k = 100 against 0.0570 measured. Any reported effect smaller than that floor is below the instrument's resolution and cannot be distinguished from zero by adding more runs; the fix is a larger k or a different readout, not more seeds.

Does argsort(argsort(x)) compute Spearman correlation correctly?

No. argsort(argsort(x)) produces an ordinal rank with no tie correction, so when the target has repeated values, which is common for log-dilution assay readouts, every correlation computed from it is biased. The correct form averages ranks within ties, as scipy.stats.rankdata does. Recomputing an entire programme of 40 models with tie-corrected ranks moved results by at most +0.009 and reordered none of them, so the bias is usually small, but it is a function of how many ties the target carries and should be measured rather than assumed.

How reliable are pooled antimicrobial peptide MIC labels?

Split-half Spearman reliability is 0.7877 with a standard deviation of 0.0067 over 200 random splits of GRAMPA peptides having four or more unmodified micromolar measurements, which Spearman-Brown corrects to 0.8812, implying a ceiling of 0.9387 against the pooled label. That ceiling should be quoted against the pooled label and not as a ceiling on any model, because 74.2% of resolvable variance is between organisms rather than between technical replicates. Between-organism Spearman is 0.596 at the median over 491 pairs, while within-organism ceilings run 0.9590 to 0.9815, higher than the pooled figure.

How do I check my own machine-learning evaluation for train/test leakage?

Compare every held-out sequence against every training sequence, not against cluster representatives, and report the fraction of held-out rows whose closest training match exceeds your identity threshold. Silicyte publishes a free browser tool at https://silicyte.com/check.html that runs four checks on a CSV with no account and no upload: sequence overlap by Levenshtein ratio, prediction spread against the resolution floor, tie-corrected versus ordinal rank correlation, and exact split membership. The file is processed locally in the browser.
